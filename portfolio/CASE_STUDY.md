# Case Study — Global Logistics Performance Analytics

## Problem
Nearshoring increased strategic attention on Mexico's role in North American supply chains, yet the course project observed a declining Logistics Performance Index score. The analysis examined whether Mexico's score was below global benchmarks, how the six LPI components related to the composite index, and what simple forecasting models implied about the future trajectory.

## Analysis
The project cleaned and explored global World Bank LPI data, compared Mexico with strategic-country groups, performed one-tailed z/t tests, estimated a six-variable OLS model for 2023, and generated Exponential Smoothing and ARIMA forecasts through 2033.

## Statistical result
Mexico's 2023 LPI score was 2.90 versus a global mean near 3.00. The one-tailed z and t tests produced p-values of 0.0236 and 0.0250: evidence at the 5% threshold, but not at 1%.

## Regression
The 2023 six-component model explained 99.7% of LPI variation. Because the LPI score is itself a composite of these dimensions, the result is interpreted as index-structure evidence rather than a standalone predictive breakthrough.

## Forecasting
Both fitted approaches projected decline. Exponential Smoothing fell much more sharply; ARIMA(1,1,1) projected a gentler decline from about 2.79 in 2025 to 2.67 in 2033 and showed widening uncertainty.

## Business interpretation
The project argued that LPI scores should be interpreted alongside real-world logistics investment, nearshoring activity, digital visibility and methodological changes. The portfolio version intentionally separates that contextual hypothesis from what the statistical models themselves establish.

## Why this project is portfolio-worthy
It demonstrates the ability to combine global supply-chain context with data cleaning, statistical inference, regression, forecasting, uncertainty, and disciplined interpretation.
